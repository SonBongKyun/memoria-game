## Purpose and canon boundary

This atlas is the playable Part I reference for the ten-chapter route from Rim Forest to BL-07. It expands the world only where that expansion sharpens an existing choice, relationship, or encounter. It does not replace the Master Bible or reveal Part II answers early.

The governing rule is simple: memory is personal experience made materially valuable. The Authority turns it into currency, fuel, and evidence; ordinary people turn it into debt, shelter, testimony, and care. Every location should show one consequence of that conflict.

## The pressure around memory

| Rule | Human consequence | On-screen expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A person may burn only deeply integrated memories. | Bought ampoules can comfort, intoxicate, or confuse, but they cannot give a buyer Arrel's combat power. | Verdan deals, tactical items, and dialogue distinguish a lived memory from a borrowed one. |
| Burning emits a unique combustion signature. | Arrel's extraordinary efficiency is also a tracking flare. | Belt routes become dangerous after a burn; the Rim and Seam offer imperfect cover, not immunity. |
| Broken ampoules become Ash Rain. | The weather carries unresolved emotional pressure without returning a whole life. | Ch1 ash, coast haze, and small NPC memories can feel familiar without becoming exposition dumps. |
| Official records are powerful because they are centralized. | A person can be made socially absent by a badge cancellation, ledger edit, or missing entry. | Authority civilians speak in stamps, routes, and categories rather than magic surveillance. |
| Record-tree paper is physically fragile but outside the Monolith. | Resistance depends on hand copies, witnesses, and people willing to carry a page. | Tobias's Blank Book and the BL-07 seed form a material alternative to official memory. |

## Character world contracts

### Arrel — the cost must stay specific

Arrel is not a generic mana user. His sword is ordinary steel marked by the residue of old burns; its strength comes from the person paying the price. Whenever he burns, identify the memory category and the immediate human loss. The world reacts to his signature as a beacon, which makes restraint as dramatic as force.

### Elia — anchoring has an ethic

Elia's role is not healing magic. She protects continuity through touch, sensory detail, and repeated shared rituals. Her presence lets a burned memory leave residue, but that residue is an afterimage, not a reversal. Her major scenes ask whether survival is enough when the self is changing.

### Tobias — records can be care

Tobias proves that writing is neither automatically oppressive nor automatically liberating. Authority ledgers reduce people to categories; his copied Blank Book preserves context, contradiction, and consent. His notes should reveal an actionable route, cost, or witness instead of merely explaining lore.

### Sable — a refuge is maintained, not discovered

Sable has survived a Void Hole and knows that survival does not make someone whole. Her authority comes from keeping practical systems alive: lantern rotations, food, gate watches, and the obligation to remember a neighbor's name. Her caution should be legible as leadership, not mystery-box withholding.

### Kairos — classification versus responsibility

Kairos initially observes Arrel as a subject and a probability. His character pressure is the gap between accurately describing suffering and accepting responsibility for it. His art and dialogue should preserve restraint: black authority tailoring, record-ink blue, and a gaze that calculates before it condemns.

### Nera and Seric — two faces of institutional harm

Nera makes absence through procedure: badge invalidation, route denial, and a finished form. Seric makes it physical: orders, containment, and the moment an Eraser stops trying to capture. They should never be interchangeable generic villains. Nera is exact and bloodless; Seric is functional and direct.

### Veil and the Echo Shell — personhood after revision

Veil carries the possibility that an edited or damaged person may still choose. An Echo Shell carries the inverse: a person-shaped remainder is not a person restored, yet it can still be owed witness. Both threads prepare the BL-07 choice without claiming that memory is a simple soul-substitute.

## Presentation rules

- Use charcoal-line, muted painterly illustration with deliberate blue, violet, amber, and parchment accents. Keep value groups calm and readable; do not add film grain or noisy overlay texture.
- Character shots prioritize face, silhouette, hands, and one story prop. The prop must be functional: Arrel's unadorned sword or burnt-blue residue, Elia's anchor motif, Tobias's notebook, Sable's ward token, Kairos/Nera/Seric's record materials.
- A place earns a new prop, NPC, or illustration only when it answers a local social question. More objects are not worldbuilding unless they change what the player understands or chooses.
- The final tone is not hopelessness. The world is cruel because memory can be owned; it becomes livable whenever someone witnesses, copies, shares, or refuses to reduce another person to a record.
